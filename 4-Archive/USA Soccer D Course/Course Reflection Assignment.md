## Personal Growth
- What is one thing about yourself that helped you learn in this course?

I try to check my ego at the door recognizing that every person in the room has something they can teach me. I'm also curious and constantly looking to simply improve because the question is not who knows the most it's can I get better?

- How did your team or coaching situation affect what you learned?

This is the first time I have jumped into the high school age pool in 20 years so I see it all from a vastly different perspective than my first go round leaving ALOT to learn.

## Coaching Experience
- What is something new you tried as a coach because of this course?

Mic'd up myself when talking to the boys. I usually do not like people hearing what I say inside the huddle but back up to the personal growth thing, while it's scary it's also a great way to get feedback and improve.


- Did this course change how you think about your coaching job or team? How?

It reinforced the structure and preparation needed to be effective, especially with this age! 


## Coaching Philosophy
- Did you learn a new idea about coaching that you want to use?

Many of the high-level fundamentals I have been exposed to multiple times, but below that there were several new ideas presented by both Bryan and peers in the class which I tried to remember and absorb. They are not so much concepts (eg game realistic environment, play/practice/play, etc) but ideas around execution and, again, planning & preparation.

- Is there a value or belief you want to add to your coaching approach?

Player first approach. I think I have done that in the past but this course reinforces/reminds me how important that is. New ideas or perspective in the development vs competition calculus. There was also a class a few weeks ago where we talked about celebrating kids little victories and since then that idea has really embedded itself in my brain and I have tried to integrate that more and more into my style of coaching.

## Motivation for Coaching
- Did anything in this course make you more excited or interested in coaching? What?

Listening to all the other coaches reminds me of where I have been and how lucky I am to be at the high school level, reaping the rewards of the years of hard work of these coaches.

- Did you learn something new about why coaching or youth soccer is important?

Increasingly I believe that while everyone can jump into the game at any time and find joy in the game, the wholistic usa soccer approach applied to youth, starting at our youngest ages, is exactly the kind of infrastructure we need to start turning usa soccer into a legit world contender. It's the kind of thing that was totally absent when I grew up so being a part of the grass roots system shows how important a mature, structured approach to the technical, tactical, phsychological and physical aspects of the game are so important.

## Player-Centered Coaching
- What is one new thing you learned about focusing on your players?

Meeting players where they are, providing opportunites to compete and push themself, keeping them engaged and maximizing touches and decisions in every activity. These might not be new things I learned but they were reinforced and brought back to the forefront for me.

- What is one way you'll help or listen to your players more after this course?

A balance of leading by example, guide using questions and/or invite player input into the game or training session

## Course Goals and Next Steps
- Did you reach your main goal for this course? Why or why not?
Yeah, I think so. If the goal is to help build up or train coaches to create effective, player-centered learning environments, then yeah, this certainlhy helped.

- What is the most important thing you learned that you'll use as a coach?

The boys are always watching. Your behavior, language, posture, attitude matters more than you know!

- What is one thing you want to try next as a coach?

1. Season theme (eg this year is "direction") that we can build on and refer back to all season long.

2. Starting something called "the monday huddle", or something like that, where the captains and 1 selected player meet to talk about the previous week and goals for the upcoming week. We did something like that for football (called "Quarterback Club") when I was young and it always pushed me to try harder to get a spot at the table. And when I did, it was so cool to see how the coaches talked and planned and then when they asked for my opinion, it really made an impression.

## Future Learning Goals and Application
- How will you use what you learned in your next practice or game?
 
Very practically speaking, I will use the drill I made up for the first homework assignment tomorrow night as the practice part of a play/practice/play format which has increasingly become my standard format (though I would call it warmup/play/practice/play)

- What are you curious to learn more about after this course?

I want more strategic and tactical education! I know how I see the game and I try to push myself to continue learning but it's hard when all I have is EPL games and youtube videos. A place to talk strategy and tactical execution would be awesome.

- What is one thing you want to get better at as a coach?

Observing and analyzing the game. I can certainly get caught watching (enjoying) the game too frequently. As a player I was so immersed I could see the game before it happened. I could analyze so quickly; it was so natural. As a coach, I struggle with that. Perhaps it's because I'm at a different vantage point far removed from actually moving on the pitch? Whatever it is, I still feel like I need to be better.
